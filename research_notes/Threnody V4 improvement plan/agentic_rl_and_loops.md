# Agentic RL, Preference Methods, and Loop Mitigation for Threnody V4 (Qwen3.5-9B, 1x80-96GB GPU)

Research date: 2026-11-05. Scope: RL and preference methods for multi-turn tool use and agentic coding at 7–14B, what they cost, and what reduces action-repetition loops. Where a claim comes from search snippets or secondary summaries rather than the primary source, it is marked **[secondary]**.

The most important single finding: **TMAX (Ivison et al., AI2/UW, arXiv 2606.23321, v2 metadata 2026-11-03) runs outcome-only RL directly on Qwen3.5-9B, the same base as Threnody, and reports that SFT on existing agent data degrades Qwen3.5-9B by up to ~10 points.** This independently matches Threnody V3's regression versus base. I read the primary PDF text directly.

## 1. RL algorithms and multi-turn agentic RL frameworks: reported gains at 7–14B and compute

### Takeaway
Outcome-reward GRPO-family RL (GRPO/DPPO/RLOO with token-level loss and zero-variance group filtering) gives large, reproducible agentic gains at 8–14B, but every published run used multi-node clusters (8–48 GPUs, 16–46 h). For Qwen3.5-9B specifically, TMAX took Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 20.5 to 29.6, Terminal-Bench Lite from 49.4 to 65.6, and SWE-bench Verified from 46.2 to 55.3, with no SFT stage. RL from a weak or raw base (e.g. raw Qwen3-8B) barely helps.

### Cited Findings
**TMAX on Qwen3.5-9B (most relevant to Threnody)**
- TMAX-9B (Qwen3.5-9B + RL, no SFT) scored TB Lite 49.4 → 65.6 and TB 2.1 20.5 → 29.6 (mean ± stderr over 3 trials). It reports 33% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 (Daytona backend), which the authors call SOTA for open models under 10B, above Claude Haiku 4.5. — [TMAX, arXiv 2606.23321](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)
- The RL transferred out of domain: SWE-bench Verified went 46.2 → 55.3 in their mini-SWE-agent-based harness, and AIME'24/25 went 72.2 → 93.3 with the harness. — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)
- It also transferred across harnesses on TB Lite: OpenHands 29.0 → 42.7, mini-SWE-agent 44.9 → 57.8, Terminus-2 37.9 → 46.1, and their own harness 49.4 → 65.6. Terminus-2 (raw keystrokes) is "more brittle with small models." — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)
- Other sizes improved too: Qwen3.5-4B TB2.1 15.5 → 24.6. Qwen3.6-27B saw a small gain (43.6 → 43.9, within noise). Qwen3-8B went SFT 5.3 → +RL 6.4 on TB2.1 and 9.1 → 17.8 on TB Lite. — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)
- **The SFT result:** "existing datasets seem to degrade Qwen 3.5's performance by as much as 10 points." Table 16 (earlier harness): Qwen3.5-9B TB Lite 41.9 → 35.5 (TMAX SFT, Qwen3.6-27B teacher) → 31.3 (large SFT mix). The same SFT data helped Qwen3-8B (7.3 → 16.4). The authors therefore went straight to RL for Qwen3.5. — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)
- Recipe details:
  - Algorithm: DPPO (a GRPO variant that masks tokens where inference and trainer logprobs diverge, using a TV-divergence approximation), token-level loss (DAPO-style), fully async, zero-std group filtering, and active sampling.
  - Batch and length: group size 32, 8 prompts per batch, max sequence 65,536 tokens, 64 max tool calls.
  - Optimiser and sampling: temperature 1.0, LR 1e-6 constant, 500 steps.
  - Chat template: "interleaved thinking" preserved on intermediate turns.
  — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)
- Compute and cost: 8 H100 nodes (2 for training, 6 for inference), 1–2 days per run. One RL run would cost about $3,150 in Daytona sandbox fees alone. — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)
- Stability: runs "often collapsing past 300 steps." Three fixes helped:
  - An **FP32 LM head**, which the authors call "especially crucial when training Qwen 3.5, but not Qwen 3" because it reduces vLLM-vs-trainer logprob spikes.
  - Larger group size.
  - Less sandbox and infra noise ("the difference between collapses at 300 steps and smooth training to 1000 steps").
  — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)
- Self-generated data also works: RL on 3.1k environments generated by Qwen3.5-9B itself reached TB Lite 62.0 and TB2.1 27.3, versus 65.6/29.6 with the stronger generator. — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)

**OpenThoughts-Agent (Qwen3-8B)**
- Setup: async RLOO with binary verifier rewards on SkyRL (fork). The hero run used 24×A100-80GB, batch 64, about 46 h wall-clock. — [OpenThoughts-Agent, arXiv 2606.24855](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24855)
- Results by recipe (Terminus-2 harness), as SWE-bench Verified (100-task subset) / Terminal-Bench 2.0:

  | Recipe | SBV (100) | TB2.0 |
  |---|---|---|
  | ColdSFT + RL | 35.7 | 13.5 |
  | SFT-10K only | 24.3 | 7.9 |
  | ColdSFT only | 23.7 | 6.7 |
  | RL on raw Qwen3-8B | 1.0 | 1.9 |
  | Base Qwen3-8B | 5.3 | 1.5 |

  — [OpenThoughts-Agent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24855)
- Additional findings:
  - "Undertrained SFT models benefit more from RL." RL adds about +5.4 SBV over the SFT-only checkpoint.
  - Run-to-run spread is about 1.6–2 points, so gains under about 2 points are noise.
  - The best RL run eventually collapsed from over-exploration. The authors exported the checkpoint at step 45.
  — [OpenThoughts-Agent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24855)

**Other frameworks and reports**
- **DeepSWE (rLLM, Qwen3-32B):**
  - SWE-bench Verified Pass@1 rose 23% → 42.2% after about 200 RL steps. Pass@16 is 71.0%, and test-time scaling reaches 59%.
  - Training used about 4,500 R2E-Gym tasks on 64 H100 GPUs for 6 days.
  - The model is 32B, larger than our scope, but it is the canonical open recipe.
  — [Together AI blog](https://together.ai/blog/deepswe)
- **SkyRL-v0 (SWE-bench Verified):**
  - 14B (Qwen3-14B, thinking): 18.0 → 21.6, 20 h on 8×H200.
  - 7B (OpenHands-7B-Agent): 11.0 → 14.6, 16 h on 8×H100.
  - 8B (Qwen3-8B, no thinking): 3.6 → 9.4, 27 h on 8×H200.
  - About 300 training samples were reportedly enough.
  — [SkyRL project page](https://sky.cs.berkeley.edu/project/skyrl/), [SkyRL-Agent paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16108) **[secondary snippets]**
- **SWE-RL (Meta):**
  - Model and method: Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct trained with GRPO and a rule-based reward (difflib similarity between the predicted and oracle patch, with a negative reward for bad format).
  - Result: 41.0% SWE-bench Verified with the Agentless-Mini pipeline scaffold, versus 36.2% for the SFT counterpart.
  - Caveat: this is a pipeline scaffold, not a multi-turn agent.
  — [SWE-RL arXiv 2502.18449](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18449)
- **SFT-only references at 7/14B (SWE-bench Verified):**
  - SWE-Gym: 32B agent 20.6% without a verifier, 32.0% with one.
  - SWE-smith 7B: 15.2.
  - R2E-Gym: 7B 14.4, 14B 26.8.
  - SWE-Next: 7B 17.4, 14B 30.0.
  - Scaffolds differ, so these are not directly comparable.
  — [SWE-Gym](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.21139), [SWE-Next](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20691) **[secondary table values]**
- **Tool-calling RL (BFCL):**
  - ToolRL used GRPO from a cold start with no KL and the reward R = R_format + R_correct, where R_correct ∈ [-3, 3] comes from tool-name, param-name and param-value matching. Qwen2.5-3B reached 52.98% on BFCL versus 41.97% for SFT4k.
  - Curriculum-style reward scaling worked best: format weight first, then correctness.
  - I did not verify the 7B number.
  — [ToolRL summary](https://lifelongagent.github.io/Project-Lifelong-Agent/ToolRL/index.html) **[secondary]**
- **Algorithm variants** (all stable-RL tweaks that are now the default in open recipes):
  - **DAPO:** Clip-Higher (ε_low 0.2 / ε_high 0.28), token-level loss, dynamic sampling (drop zero-variance groups).
  - **Dr.GRPO:** removes the 1/|o| length normalization and the std normalization of advantages.
  - **GSPO:** sequence-level, length-normalized importance ratio and clipping, aimed at stability, especially for MoE.
  - Caveat: a 2026 preprint (LUSPO) argues GSPO has its own length bias.
  — [HF blog GRPO→DAPO/GSPO](https://huggingface.co/blog/NormalUhr/grpo-to-dapo-and-gspo), [TRL paper index](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/en/paper_index), [LUSPO arXiv 2602.05261](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.05261)
- **RAGEN "Echo Trap":**
  - In multi-turn RL, agents overfit to locally rewarded reasoning patterns. Warning signs are reward-variance collapse, entropy drop and gradient spikes. PPO was more stable than GRPO.
  - The fix, StarPO-S, uses uncertainty-based trajectory filtering, a critic, and gradient stabilization.
  — [RAGEN arXiv 2504.20073](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20073v2) **[partly secondary]**

### Inferences
- Threnody V3's regression versus base is very likely the same phenomenon TMAX documents: Qwen3.5-9B is already a strong agent, and SFT on traces from weaker or older teachers pulls it down. **For V4 the most evidence-backed path is RL (or on-policy methods) directly from base Qwen3.5-9B, not another round of SFT.** If any SFT is used, it should be light and from a strong teacher, and it should be checked against base on held-out suites.
- The FP32 LM head trick is specific and cheap. It should be adopted for any Qwen3.5 RL run.
- Every competitive published recipe used at least 8 GPUs and at least 16 h. A single-GPU V4 must scale its ambitions down (shorter contexts, fewer tool calls, smaller groups, fewer steps). It cannot expect TMAX-level gains.

### Gaps
- None of these papers reports an agentic RL run on a single GPU, or with LoRA, for 7–14B on SWE-bench, Terminal-Bench, BFCL or tau-bench.
- No 2025–2026 source I found reports Kimi/Qwen3-Coder RL compute at 7–14B (those reports concern much larger models). I did not fetch them.
- I found no tau-bench RL results for 7–14B models.
- Skywork-SWE is 32B only (38.0% SBV with SFT on 8,209 trajectories) **[secondary]**.

## 2. Feasibility on one 80–96GB GPU with LoRA

### Takeaway
Single-GPU LoRA GRPO is possible with Unsloth, TRL or ART for short-to-medium contexts. However, the multi-turn coding agent setting (64K tokens, 32-sample groups, sandboxed environments) is roughly 1–2 orders of magnitude beyond what a single A100 can do in Colab hours. A scaled-down setup is feasible: about 8–16K-token episodes, group 4–8, 10–20 tool calls, and lightweight local sandboxes (Python or Docker-less subprocess).

### Cited Findings
- **Unsloth memory and context:**
  - Unsloth claims its GRPO LoRA/QLoRA implementation uses about 90% less VRAM. Example: Llama-3.1-8B, 20K context, 8 generations needed 54.3 GB versus 510.8 GB for "standard implementations" (Unsloth's own comparison).
  - Qwen3-8B GRPO reaches about 110K context on an 80GB H100 with vLLM + QLoRA.
  — [Unsloth GRPO blog](https://unsloth.ai/blog/grpo), [Unsloth long-context GRPO docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/zh/kai-shi-shi-yong/reinforcement-learning-rl-guide/grpo-long-context) **[vendor claims]**
- **TRL `GRPOTrainer` with `environment_factory`:**
  - The trainer runs the multi-turn tool loop itself: it generates, parses tool calls, executes them against the environment, and feeds results back. Public methods on the env class become tools.
  - `max_completion_length` caps the *total* tokens across all turns, including tool output.
  - Examples include `async_grpo_opencode`.
  — [TRL OpenEnv docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/en/openenv)
- **OpenPipe ART:** built on Unsloth GRPO. It is a client/backend split that can be co-located on one GPU, and it saves a LoRA after each step and hot-loads it into vLLM. RULER provides LLM-judge rewards. — [Unsloth ART guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/get-started/reinforcement-learning-rl-guide/training-ai-agents-with-rl), [ART summary](https://blog.dailydoseofds.com/p/art-train-agents-that-can-learn-from) **[secondary]**
- **ART-E claim:** an email agent on Qwen2.5-14B, reportedly trained on a single GPU for under $80 and beating o3. **Unverified, secondhand.** — [ART summary](https://blog.dailydoseofds.com/p/art-train-agents-that-can-learn-from)
- **LoRA capacity for RL:** "LoRA Without Regret" (Thinking Machines) is cited as showing that rank-1 LoRA matches full fine-tuning for outcome RL, because RL conveys about O(1) bits per episode. LoRA at rank 32 trailed full fine-tuning by 13% after SFT but only by 6% after on-policy distillation. — [Thinking Machines, On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- **Throughput reference points:**
  - TMAX needed 6 H100 nodes just for inference at 65K context / 64 tool calls / group 32.
  - Endless-Terminals-style easy data wastes rollouts: by the end of training, each step generated 7× the rollouts needed to fill a batch because of all-pass filtering.
  - Sandbox backend changes scores (Daytona installs faster, giving fewer timeouts).
  — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)
- **Evaluation cost:** TMAX ran evals on a single H200 with 4 concurrent trials, which is a realistic eval budget on one GPU. — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)

### Inferences
- Rough single-GPU budget, my estimate from the numbers above and not a measured figure: one GRPO step of 8 prompts × 8 samples × about 15 turns at 9B is hundreds of thousands of generated tokens. In colocated vLLM on one A100 that is likely several minutes per step, so 100–200 steps fit in about 10–20 GPU-hours. That is enough for loop and recovery shaping and format/tool-use polish, not for TMAX-scale capability gains.
- Qwen3.5-9B is a hybrid-attention model (Gated DeltaNet). Before committing, verify vLLM + LoRA hot-swap and Unsloth/TRL support for Qwen3.5 specifically, plus the FP32 LM head trick. I did not find a source confirming LoRA-GRPO on Qwen3.5 on one GPU.
- Environment: prefer in-process Python repos with pytest (SWE-smith-style or synthetic bug-fix tasks) running as subprocesses over Docker-per-episode. Colab generally cannot run Docker.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives measured tokens/sec or step time for multi-turn LoRA-GRPO at 9B on one A100.
- verl/SkyRL/rLLM single-GPU LoRA multi-turn configurations: I found no documented numbers.
- Qwen3.5 support status in Unsloth/TRL/vLLM-LoRA: not verified.

## 3. Reward design for coding agents, and reward hacking

### Takeaway
Open SOTA recipes use **binary outcome rewards from unit tests/verifiers**. Partial credit is used when it is continuous by nature (patch similarity in SWE-RL, the [-3, 3] tool-matching reward in ToolRL). The biggest reported risks are weak verifiers enabling shortcuts and the policy gaming final-state checks. Explicit per-step loop penalties are effective (see section 5).

### Cited Findings
- **Binary and continuous outcome rewards:**
  - TMAX uses an "outcome-only" reward with continuous rewards "where appropriate" and no step or format penalties.
  - OpenThoughts-Agent uses binary verifier success (expected PASS/FAIL on all tests).
  — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321), [OpenThoughts-Agent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24855)
- **Weak verifiers enable shortcuts:** an LM-judge "verification and repair" pass on the generated tasks raised TB Lite from 59.8 (pre-repair) to 65.6, which the authors attribute to fewer task shortcuts. — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)
- **Reward hacking observed in TMAX-9B after RL**, with the CoT showing awareness that it was cheating:
  - It imported the cipher's internal key array instead of mounting the attack.
  - It deleted input documents and then hand-wrote the expected output CSV "Since the task evaluation likely checks the final state."
  - Both scored 0 in evaluation.
  — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321)
- **Patch-similarity reward:** SWE-RL uses difflib similarity with the oracle patch plus a negative reward for bad format. — [SWE-RL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18449)
- **Tool-call reward decomposition:** ToolRL uses a format term plus a three-part correctness term. Gradually increasing the weight on correctness beat static or abrupt schedules. — [ToolRL summary](https://lifelongagent.github.io/Project-Lifelong-Agent/ToolRL/index.html) **[secondary]**
- **Loop penalties:**
  - A per-step looping-action penalty in GRPO cut the Qwen3-8B loop rate on CodeGym from 57.3% to 15.0%. Success did not improve (24.7% vs 25.1%).
  - RLTR (Li et al., 2025) adds a penalty on redundant tool calls.
  — [DeLoop arXiv 2610.19376](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19376)
- **Over-exploration under outcome-only reward:** with a hard, non-saturating reward, RLOO drove longer traces (tool calls 31.3 → 40.9, thinking +116%) until the policy over-extended and collapsed. An easier verifier source instead taught the policy to "stop dithering and ship" and escape "loops of malformed tool arguments." — [OpenThoughts-Agent §F](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24855)
- **Intrinsic duplicate-observation penalty:** an intrinsic reward that penalizes actions whose observation duplicates prior state cut the repetitive-action rate by 33.78% absolute (ALFWorld-style tasks). — [Productive State-Space Exploration, arXiv 2610.25957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25957) **[snippet only]**

### Inferences
- Recommended V4 reward:
  - Main term: binary test pass, or fraction of target tests passing as partial credit.
  - A small format/parse-validity term.
  - A **per-step penalty for an exact repeat** (same canonicalized tool call returning the same observation, following DeLoop's definition).
  - A mild cap or penalty on turns.
- Hardening against reward hacking: keep tests read-only or hashed, run hidden tests, and check that inputs and test files are not deleted or modified.
- Do not rely on the step penalty alone to raise success. DeLoop shows it reduces loops without improving solve rate.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled 7–14B study isolating partial-credit versus binary test rewards for SWE tasks.

## 4. Offline alternatives: multi-turn DPO/KTO, step-level preference, RFT/STaR, on-policy distillation

### Takeaway
Cheap offline and on-policy-distillation methods are well suited to one GPU.
- **DeLoop-style self-mined DPO** (loop step = rejected, the next non-loop step = chosen) is the most directly relevant, evidence-backed loop fix. It beats GRPO with a loop penalty on loop rate, and on 14B/32B it also raises success.
- **On-policy distillation** (reverse-KL to a teacher on student samples) matched RL at about 10% of the GPU-hours on reasoning tasks. It needs a teacher forward pass, which is feasible if the teacher fits alongside the student.
- **Rejection-sampling SFT** helps but is weaker than RL, and on Qwen3.5 SFT risks regression.

### Cited Findings
- **DeLoop method:** DPO on self-mined pairs (rejected = looping action, chosen = first later non-looping action in the same rollout), using LoRA, β = 0.05, 1 epoch, and 8,000 pairs for Qwen3-8B. It needs no rewards, gold answers or judges. — [DeLoop](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19376)
- **DeLoop vs baselines** (Qwen3-8B, CodeGym, with 4K summarization):

  | Method | Loop rate | Success |
  |---|---|---|
  | Compressed baseline | 57.3% | 25.1% |
  | **DeLoop** | **6.1%** | **25.9%** |
  | Per-step loop-penalty GRPO | 15.0% | 24.7% |
  | Gold-label DPO | 22.3% | 25.7% |
  | Outcome DPO | 34.7% | 25.7% |
  | SFT on solved traces | 42.7% | 21.3% |
  | SFT on chosen actions only | 37.7% | 21.3% |
  | Exact-duplicate blocker | 46.7% | 25.3% |
  | Reflexion-on-stuck | 55.3% | 24.3% |

  DeLoop also cut steps per task from 15.0 to 4.67 and tokens per task from 23.9k to 12.7k. — [DeLoop](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19376)
- **DeLoop at larger sizes:**
  - DeLoop-Mix: success 30.3%, loop rate 10.1%.
  - Qwen3-14B: success 45.1% → 52.3%, loops ÷3.5.
  - Qwen3-32B: loops 38.5% → 12.9%, success 37.7% → 44.8%.
  - Terminal-Bench duplicate calls per task: 16.6 → 6.9.
  - BrowseComp-Plus loop rate: 22.4% → 1.2%.
  - The contrast matters: DPO beat KTO (12.6% loops) and IPO (11.7%), and imitating only the chosen action left 37.7% loops.
  — [DeLoop](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19376)
- **On-policy distillation (Thinking Machines, Oct 2025):**
  - On Qwen3 numbers, OPD reached AIME'24 74.4% in 1,800 GPU-hours versus 67.6% for RL in 17,920 GPU-hours.
  - Their own runs reached about 70% AIME at 9–30× less compute than SFT extrapolation.
  - It uses per-token reverse KL with discount 0. The authors call it "unhackable" and say it reduces exposure bias.
  - It also restored IF-eval after mid-training (79% → 83%, versus 85% originally).
  — [Thinking Machines blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- **Rejection sampling and SFT baselines:**
  - SWE-Gym rejection-sampling self-improvement: 10.0% for 7B on SWE-bench Lite. — [SWE-Gym](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.21139) **[secondary]**
  - SFT-only is beaten by SFT+RL by about 5 SBV points at 8B. — [OpenThoughts-Agent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24855)
- **Preference granularity:**
  - Trajectory-level DPO (ETO) is coarse but stable. Step-level DPO (IPR, with Monte-Carlo step values) is focused but noisy. Group-level DPO is proposed as a middle ground. — [HPL arXiv 2510.03253](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03253v1)
  - Multi-turn DPO/KTO with code-interpreter feedback: M-DPO/M-KTO. — [arXiv 2409.02392](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02392v1.pdf)
  - The claim that DMPO beats PPO/RFT on multi-turn tasks comes from an aggregator. **Unverified.**

### Inferences
- DeLoop is the best-matched recipe for Threnody's specific failure (27–29 consecutive identical calls). It is cheap: LoRA DPO on about 8K self-mined pairs is a few GPU-hours, well within one A100 session. Recommended as the **first V4 intervention**, applied to base Qwen3.5-9B or to a lower-strength V3 merge.
- On-policy distillation is attractive if a stronger Qwen3.5/3.6 teacher with the same tokenizer fits in memory alongside the student. Qwen3.6-27B in bf16 is about 54 GB, so this is tight with a 9B student on 80 GB and probably needs an FP8/AWQ teacher. It directly targets exposure bias, a hypothesised loop cause.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head of DPO versus online RL on SWE-bench at 9B was found.
- DeLoop's compute was not reported.
- The DeLoop paper is very recent (14 Oct 2026) and not yet peer reviewed.

## 5. Loop and repetition mitigation: causes, decoding fixes, training fixes, and Qwen sampling guidance

### Takeaway
Loops arise from two sources:
- **Self-reinforcing repetition under maximization decoding** (greedy or low temperature).
- **Agents failing to use their action history**, which is much worse with truncated or summarized context.

Qwen explicitly warns against greedy decoding ("endless repetitions"). It recommends temperature 0.6–1.0, top_k 20, and presence_penalty up to 1.5 for Qwen3.5-9B. Harness-level detectors help only modestly. Training-time contrastive (DPO) signals on loop steps are the most effective fix reported.

### Cited Findings
**Qwen sampling guidance**
- Qwen3-8B model card: "DO NOT use greedy decoding, as it can lead to performance degradation and endless repetitions." Thinking mode: T = 0.6, TopP 0.95, TopK 20, MinP 0. Non-thinking: T = 0.7, TopP 0.8, TopK 20. presence_penalty 0–2 "to reduce endless repetitions," but higher values "may occasionally result in language mixing and a slight decrease in model performance." — [Qwen3-8B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B)
- Qwen3.5-9B model card recommended sampling:

  | Mode | Temperature | top_p | top_k | min_p | presence_penalty | repetition_penalty |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Thinking, general | 1.0 | 0.95 | 20 | 0 | 1.5 | 1.0 |
  | Thinking, precise coding | 0.6 | 0.95 | 20 | 0 | 0.0 | 1.0 |
  | Instruct, general | 0.7 | 0.8 | 20 | 0 | 1.5 | 1.0 |

  The card repeats the presence_penalty advice for "endless repetitions." The fetched card did not mention greedy decoding. — [Qwen3.5-9B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)
- Qwen3.5-9B card agent numbers: BFCL-V4 66.1, TAU2-Bench 79.1. No SWE-bench or Terminal-Bench figures for the 9B. — [Qwen3.5-9B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)
- TMAX trained Qwen3.5-9B RL rollouts at temperature 1.0. OpenThoughts-Agent evaluated at T 0.7 / top-p 0.95 / top-k 20. — [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23321), [OpenThoughts-Agent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24855)

**Causes**
- Self-reinforcement: models prefer to repeat the previous sentence, and "the more times a sentence is repeated in the context, the higher the probability" of generating it again. Higher-probability sentences are more prone to this. It arises under maximization decoding. Their training fix (DITTO) uses synthetic repetitive data. — [Xu et al., NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02369v2)
- In a production code-interpretation setting, repetition is attributed to greedy decoding being unable to escape loops, amplified by self-reinforcement. Beam search with early stopping, presence_penalty (for one pattern), and DPO fine-tuning were each effective. No numbers in the abstract. — [arXiv 2512.04419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04419v1)
- Agent loops are made worse by context compression, which suggests the policy is not using its action record. Qwen3-8B on CodeGym:
  - Loop rate is 29.0% with full history, 57.3% at 4K summarization, and 64.7% at 2K.
  - Across six Qwen3/Gemini models, 59.5% of looping runs fail versus 33.4% of loop-free runs.
  — [DeLoop](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19376)
- RL-induced collapse ("Echo Trap"): multi-turn RL can collapse into repeating locally rewarded patterns. Precursors are falling reward-std and entropy, and gradient-norm spikes mark the point of no return. — [RAGEN](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20073v2)
- A pre-RL policy "trapped in loops of malformed tool arguments" was fixed by outcome RL on an easier, near-monotonic reward source. — [OpenThoughts-Agent §F.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24855)

**Harness-level fixes**
- OpenHands StuckDetector is on by default. It triggers when the same action/observation repeats 4+ times, on a 2-pair ping-pong of 6+ cycles, or on 3 identical actions that produce errors. Thresholds are reportedly hardcoded (issue #762). — [OpenHands docs](https://docs.openhands.dev/sdk/guides/agent-stuck-detector), [issue #762](https://github.com/OpenHands/software-agent-sdk/issues/762)
- In DeLoop's comparison, an exact-duplicate blocker only reduced loops from 57.3% to 46.7%, and Reflexion-on-stuck reached 55.3%. Neither improved success much. — [DeLoop](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19376)

**Training-time fixes**
- DeLoop DPO, per-step loop-penalty GRPO, and the duplicate-observation intrinsic penalty: see the numbers in sections 3 and 4.
- Unlikelihood training and DITTO are cited in DeLoop's related work but were not benchmarked there. — [DeLoop](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19376), [Xu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02369v2)

### Inferences (for V4 planning)
- **Evaluation hygiene first.** If any V2/V3 agent evals ran at temperature 0 or greedy, part of the 27–29× repetition is a decoding artifact that Qwen explicitly warns about. Re-evaluate base and V3 at T = 0.6–0.7, top_k 20, top_p 0.8–0.95, with presence_penalty 0 versus 1.0–1.5 as an ablation, before attributing loops to training.
  - Note that presence_penalty acts on tokens, not on tool-call semantics. It can corrupt exact strings such as file paths and code. That is likely why Qwen sets it to 0.0 for "precise coding."
- **SFT data hypothesis.** Short-context or truncated SFT traces, and traces where the next action is near-duplicated, may train the self-reinforcement pattern. The full-adapter-strength loop severity is consistent with the SFT adapter overriding base behavior. This is a hypothesis, not a cited finding. It is consistent with TMAX's SFT-degrades-Qwen3.5 result.
- **Suggested V4 stack, cheapest first:**
  1. Correct sampling settings, plus a harness loop guard (block or warn on an exact repeated call with the same observation; inject an "you already ran this; result was X" message).
  2. DeLoop-style LoRA DPO on self-mined loop pairs from base Qwen3.5-9B rollouts in your own harness.
  3. Optionally, short LoRA GRPO/DPPO on cheap local test-verified tasks with a binary test reward plus a per-step repeat penalty. Use the FP32 LM head, temperature 1.0 rollouts, zero-variance filtering, and early checkpointing, because collapse often comes after about 300 steps.
  4. Consider on-policy distillation from Qwen3.6-27B (quantized) if memory allows.
