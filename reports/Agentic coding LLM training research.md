# Switch bases, fix the recipe, ship Threnody

Threnody should be a **bf16 LoRA fine-tune of `Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B`**, not another Qwen2.5-Coder-7B adapter. The 9B is the only top 2026 candidate that fits entirely on an RTX 3060 12GB (Q4 is about 6.6 GB) and still leaves room for long context. It already beats a 30B-A3B thinking model on tool calling and multi-turn tasks (BFCL-V4 66.1, TAU2 79.1), and it shares the `qwen3_coder` tool format with the larger Qwen3.6-35B-A3B. That shared format keeps a "big Threnody" upgrade path open. Fable-Coder-V4's +2-point result has a clear explanation: an LR of 2e-5 (about 10x too low for LoRA), loss computed on full text including tool outputs, a 4,096-token cap that truncates most agent trajectories, and a 6-pair DPO stage that collapsed. Fixing those four problems and training on 2-4K resolved, length-filtered agent trajectories from permissively licensed sets should matter far more than any hyperparameter tuning. Kwai-Klear's mini-swe-agent set, Nemotron-Terminal-Corpus, SWE-rebench-OpenHands, and SWE-smith are the core. At measured 2026 Colab rates, 200 compute units buy about **37 A100-40GB hours**. That covers a data-and-recipe pilot, a full SFT run, a small GRPO stage on in-process unit-test tasks, and imatrix quantization, with margin for one retry. Multi-turn SWE-style RL with Docker sandboxes is not feasible on Colab. The 16GB system-RAM constraint also rules out the otherwise attractive Qwen3.6-35B-A3B with expert offload, because every working report of that setup assumes 32GB.

## Base choice turns on 16GB of system RAM, and Qwen3.5-9B wins

The 2025 shortlist (Qwen3-2507, Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B, Devstral Small 1.x) is out of date. Qwen shipped Qwen3.5 in February-March 2026 (including 35B-A3B, 27B, 9B and 4B), Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and 27B in April, and Qwen3.8-27B in August, all Apache-2.0 with 262K native context ([QwenLM GitHub](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.8)). **No official Qwen3.8 9B or 35B-A3B exists.** Community repos with names like "Qwen3.8-Distill-35B-A3B-Coder" are unofficial ([HF discussion #120](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B/discussions/120)). The 3.5/3.6/3.8 family uses a 3:1 hybrid of Gated DeltaNet linear attention and gated full attention, with thinking on by default and switchable via `enable_thinking`. On vLLM/SGLang it uses the `qwen3_coder` XML tool parser, which suits CLI harnesses directly ([Qwen3.6-35B-A3B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B)).

The vendor-reported agentic numbers set the ceiling. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B posts **SWE-bench Verified 73.4 and Terminal-Bench 2.0 51.5**, against 52.0/42.9 for Gemma 4 31B and just 17.4 SWE-V for Gemma 4 26B-A4B in the same table ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B)). Qwen3.8-27B leads the size class at **TB 2.1 73.0 and SWE-bench Pro 61.7 run in the Claude Code harness** ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B)). The alternatives trail: GLM-4.7-Flash (30B-A3B) reaches SWE-V 59.2 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-4.7-Flash)), and Devstral Small 2 (24B dense) reaches 68.0 vendor-reported but 56.4 under the open harness ([letsdatascience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/mistral-releases-devstral-2-for-code-agents-2e24c245)). All of these scores come from vendors' own scaffolds, and none was verified on an independent leaderboard.

Hardware decides the rest. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at UD-Q4_K_M (22.1 GB) runs at **38.9 tok/s on an RTX 3060 12GB with `--n-cpu-moe 24`** (9.8 GB VRAM) and holds about 36 tok/s with a 16K prompt. That test machine had **32GB DDR4**, and the same guide sets 32GB as the RAM floor for Q4 offload ([insiderllm MoE guide](https://insiderllm.com/guides/best-way-run-qwen-3-6-35b-moe-locally/)). At that setting about 12 GB of experts would sit in system RAM next to the OS, the terminal harness and an editor. On a 16GB machine that is marginal at best, and no source tested it. Dense 27B models need about 16-17 GB at Q4 and fall to single-digit tok/s when offloaded ([insiderllm](https://insiderllm.com/guides/best-local-coding-models-2026/)). That leaves **Qwen3.5-9B (Q4 ≈ 6.6 GB)** as the one strong candidate that fits the 3060 completely. Its card reports **BFCL-V4 66.1 and TAU2-Bench 79.1, against 42.4 and 41.9 for Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507** ([Qwen3.5-9B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)). The card gives no SWE-bench or Terminal-Bench score for the 9B. That is the main uncertainty, and it is also where agent-trajectory SFT has the most room to add value.

Develop the data and recipe on the 9B. If the user upgrades to 32GB RAM, or Qwen releases an official Qwen3.8 35B-A3B or 9B, port the same data to that model, since tooling, template and tool format carry over.

## Resolved, terminal-native trajectories should make up most of the mix

Fable-V4's weak spot is closed-loop sandbox work (1/4), and the fix for that is execution-grounded multi-turn data. Plenty of it is now openly licensed. The constraint is **length**: a re-tokenization of 15,000 sessions from three major sets puts the median deepest prompt at **23,840 tokens, p95 at 69,274, and 32% of sessions between 32K and 64K** ([thoughtworks card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/thoughtworks/agentic-coding-trajectories)). CoderForge has a median of 38,052 tokens ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/togethercomputer/CoderForge-Preview)). A Colab budget can only afford the short tail, so length filtering is the main curation step.

The strongest fit for a CLI harness is **`Kwai-Klear/SWE-smith-mini_swe_agent_plus-trajectories-66k`** (MIT, 65,994 trajectories, all resolved, bash-only scaffold). Klear-Agent-8B trained on it reports **39.0% SWE-bench Verified**, and solve rate scales roughly linearly with log(data size) from 1K to 66K ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Kwai-Klear/SWE-smith-mini_swe_agent_plus-trajectories-66k)). **`nvidia/Nemotron-Terminal-Corpus`** (CC-BY-4.0, about 366K terminal trajectories) is the closest match to sandbox tasks: it moved **Qwen3-8B from 2.5% to 13.0% on Terminal-Bench 2.0** ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-Terminal-Corpus)). **`nebius/SWE-rebench-openhands-trajectories`** (CC-BY-4.0) adds 32,161 successful OpenHands runs with a `resolved` flag. Its tool-call arguments are stored as strings and must be `json.loads`-ed before templating ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nebius/SWE-rebench-openhands-trajectories)). **`SWE-bench/SWE-smith-trajectories`** (MIT) offers `tool`, `xml` and `ticks` configs, and 5,017 of its trajectories trained SWE-agent-LM-32B to 40.2% ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SWE-bench/SWE-smith-trajectories)). **`open-thoughts/OpenThoughts-Agent-v1-SFT`** (Apache-2.0, about 15.2K Terminus-2 traces, max 32 turns) adds scaffold diversity ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/open-thoughts/OpenThoughts-Agent-v1-SFT)). For short examples, **`nebius/SWE-agent-trajectories`** has resolved runs averaging only **8,352 tokens** ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nebius/SWE-agent-trajectories)). NVIDIA's `Nemotron-SFT-SWE-v3` (CC-BY-4.0, 237,970 samples across OpenHands, SWE-agent and mini-SWE-agent) is the best single source of scaffold variety ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-SFT-SWE-v3)). Give lower priority to execution-free sets such as `nvidia/SWE-Zero-openhands-trajectories`, which carry no `resolved` field ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/SWE-Zero-openhands-trajectories)), and to CoderForge, which has no dataset-level license tag and a 50 GB download.

For tool calling, **`nvidia/Nemotron-Agentic-v1`** (CC-BY-4.0) provides 19,028 genuinely multi-turn `interactive_agent` rows ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-Agentic-v1)). `NousResearch/hermes-function-calling-v1` and `Team-ACE/ToolACE` are both Apache-2.0 ([Hermes](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NousResearch/hermes-function-calling-v1); [ToolACE](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Team-ACE/ToolACE)). **`nvidia/When2Call` `train_sft`** teaches when not to call a tool; never train on its `test` config ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/When2Call)). Exclude `Salesforce/APIGen-MT-5k` from an open release because it is CC-BY-NC and gated ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Salesforce/APIGen-MT-5k)). For single-turn coding reasoning, use **`nvidia/OpenCodeReasoning-2` filtered to `pass_rate == 1`** ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/OpenCodeReasoning-2)) or **`microsoft/rStar-Coder` with `is_passed`** ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/microsoft/rStar-Coder)), both CC-BY-4.0. KodCode is non-commercial.

Defensive security needs the most care. **Drop `CyberNative/Code_Vulnerability_Security_DPO`.** Its own maintainers report that DPO on it cut a coder's HumanEval+MBPP pass@1 from **59.8% to 21.5%** with no measured security gain, and that CodeShield flags 12.5% of the "chosen" answers ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CyberNative/Code_Vulnerability_Security_DPO)). Replace V4's offensive-oriented red-team data with **`Trendyol/Trendyol-Cybersecurity-Instruction-Tuning-Dataset`** (Apache-2.0, 53,202 defensive examples) ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Trendyol/Trendyol-Cybersecurity-Instruction-Tuning-Dataset)) and with **`laion/nemotron-terminal-security`** (CC-BY-4.0). The latter is the only agentic, execution-feedback security set found ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/laion/nemotron-terminal-security)). `nvidia/Nemotron-RL-Agentic-Indirect-Prompt-Injection-v1` is an RL environment for resisting injections hidden in tool outputs, not SFT data. It can supply a few hundred rolled-out examples ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-RL-Agentic-Indirect-Prompt-Injection-v1)). For replay, the Apache-licensed subsets of **`HuggingFaceTB/smoltalk2`** fit best, since it includes think and no_think tool-calling traces ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceTB/smoltalk2)). `allenai/tulu-3-sft-mixture` includes non-commercial portions and must be filtered by source ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/tulu-3-sft-mixture)).

Decontaminate by identifier, not just n-grams. The agentleak audit found **348 of 32,079 SWE-rebench-V2 tasks overlapping Multi-SWE-bench, SWE-PolyBench or SWE-bench Multilingual**, and a 13-gram filter missed overlap once problem statements had been LLM-rewritten ([agentleak](https://github.com/raimondasl/agentleak)). Match on repo+PR, base_commit and fix patch. Run an embedding check against Terminal-Bench 2.x/3.0 instructions (public at `harborframework/terminal-bench-2.0`) and against OmniAgent-Bench's 16 tasks.

## Four recipe fixes outweigh any new trick

The likely cause of V4's flat result is in its recipe, not its data. "LoRA Without Regret" finds that the optimal LoRA learning rate is **about 10x the full-fine-tune rate (fitted multiplier 9.8, about 15x for runs under 100 steps)**. With alpha/r scaling that optimum is nearly rank-independent. LoRA should target **all layers, especially MLP**, because attention-only LoRA at rank 256 underperforms MLP-only at rank 128. LoRA also degrades at large batch, so keep the effective batch small ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)). V4's 2e-5 is TRL's full-FT default, and TRL itself recommends about 1e-4 for adapters ([TRL SFT docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/sft_trainer)). Use **r=64, alpha=32 (or rsLoRA with alpha=r), all linear projections, LR 1e-4 to 2e-4 with cosine decay and 3-5% warmup, effective batch 16-32, 2-3 epochs**.

Loss masking is the second fix. With `assistant_only_loss=True` on `messages` plus a `tools` column, TRL computes loss only on assistant tokens, and it auto-patches Qwen3 templates with `{% generation %}` markers ([TRL SFT docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/sft_trainer)). In an agent trajectory most tokens are file contents and command output, so full-text loss spends most of the gradient teaching the model to predict `ls` output. Check the mask by decoding one sample where labels != -100: only reasoning, assistant text and tool-call JSON should remain. The notes did not confirm that TRL's auto-patch covers the Qwen3.5 hybrid template, so verify it by decoding. Pre-tokenized `labels` are the fallback. To keep thinking mode intact, Unsloth recommends about **75% reasoning to 25% non-reasoning data** ([Unsloth Qwen3 docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen)). Strip `reasoning_content` only where you intend non-thinking behavior, and make the training template handle earlier-turn `<think>` blocks the same way serving does. The Qwen3.6 card's `preserve_thinking` flag shows the model family supports keeping them.

Sequence length is the third fix. A 4,096 cap truncates nearly every useful trajectory. Train at **16K, with a small share up to 24K**, using gradient checkpointing. Note that TRL's `max_length` defaults to 1024, and a model passed as a string loads in float32 unless `dtype=bfloat16` is set ([TRL SFT docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/sft_trainer)). Precision is the fourth fix. Unsloth says **"4-bit QLoRA on Qwen3.5 is not recommended"** for dense or MoE models because quantization error runs higher than usual ([Unsloth Qwen3.5 docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.5/fine-tune)). Use bf16 LoRA (about 22 GB for the 9B at short context) on an A100 40GB. The L4 24GB is too tight at 16K.

Published scaling curves suggest the data volume is right. SWE-smith took a 32B coder to **28.2% Verified with 500 trajectories and 40.2% with 5,016** ([arXiv 2504.21798](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.21798)). SWE-Gym's 491 trajectories lifted Qwen2.5-Coder-32B from about 7% to **20.6%** and were still climbing ([arXiv 2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.21139v2)). Every one of these papers trains on rejection-sampled, test-passing teacher trajectories. That is what the public sets above already contain, so Threnody does not need to spend Colab units generating them.

RL is feasible only at small scale. Unsloth's Standby mode fits **Qwen3-4B GRPO on a T4 (about 14.5 GiB, about 32K KV)** and reaches 10K+ sequences for a 3B model on an A100 40GB where TRL managed about 1K ([Unsloth memory-efficient RL](https://unsloth.ai/docs/get-started/reinforcement-learning-rl-guide/memory-efficient-rl)). TRL's GRPOTrainer now defaults to **DAPO token-level loss with beta=0**. It offers GSPO via `importance_sampling_level="sequence"` and has an experimental `environment_factory` for multi-turn tool environments ([TRL GRPO docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/grpo_trainer)). LoRA matches full fine-tuning for RL even at very low rank ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)). No source shows single-GPU multi-turn SWE RL with Docker sandboxes improving a 4B-32B model, and Colab cannot run Docker. A realistic stage is **GRPO with LoRA r=16-32, LR about 5e-6 to 1e-5, 4-8 generations, 300-1,000 prompts with in-process unit tests or scripted tool tasks, 200-500 steps**. Drop prompts where every sample passes or every sample fails. V4's DPO collapse (6 pairs, 80 steps) shows why the stage needs hundreds of prompts and an eval every 25-50 steps.

Forgetting has cheap mitigations. LoRA "learns less and forgets less" than full FT ([arXiv 2405.09673](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09673v2)), and one informal replication found **10% replay recovered about 73% of forgetting** ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/javieraguilarai/the-forgetting-you-dont-measure-3h11)). Post-hoc adapter scaling is close to free. An ICML 2026 audit found that naive LoRA merging cost **-18.3 pp on HumanEval**, while Task Arithmetic at λ=0.5 stayed within one standard error of baseline ([ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/74593)). Evaluate the merged model at λ ∈ {0.5, 0.75, 1.0} and ship whichever scores best.

Colab budget, at rates measured in March 2026, is better than assumed: **T4 1.19 CU/h, L4 1.71, A100 40GB 5.40, A100 80GB 7.52, G4 (96GB Blackwell) 8.71**, or about 37 A100-40GB hours for 200 CU ([McCormick](https://mccormickml.com/2024/04/23/colab-gpus-features-and-pricing/)). Rates vary by region and demand, so check the Resources tab. Save LoRA checkpoints to Drive or a private HF repo every 25-50 steps (`hub_strategy="every_save"`) and resume with `resume_from_checkpoint=True`. The notes contain no measured 9B tokens/sec at 16K, so the run-length estimates in the plan below are inferences that the pilot must confirm.

## The hybrid architecture already removes most long-context memory pressure

Weight quantization sets most of the quality budget. On llama.cpp's Llama 3 8B scoreboard, **Q8_0 (KLD 0.0014) and Q6_K (0.0055) are effectively lossless, Q5_K_M (0.011) is near-lossless, and Q4_K_M with imatrix sits at the knee (KLD 0.028, about 92% same-top-token)**. Quality then drops steeply: IQ3_XXS has KLD 0.18 and IQ2_XXS 0.81 ([llama.cpp perplexity README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/README.md)). An imatrix cuts Q4_K_M KLD by about 10% and Q3_K_M by about 17%, and adding calibration tokens beyond about 1M showed no consistent gain. Unsloth Dynamic quants shave another 3-8% of KLD at nearly the same size. Unsloth has moved its calibration data toward agentic coding, and it warns that Wikipedia-based imatrix data inflates Wikipedia-based KLD tests ([Unsloth Dynamic 2.0](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/unsloth-dynamic-2.0-ggufs)). For an agent, a 92% same-top-token rate means roughly one in twelve tokens differs from the bf16 choice. A changed bracket or JSON key breaks a turn, so **Q5_K_M or Q6_K should be the default on the 3060, not Q4**. Build the imatrix from about 1M tokens of chat-templated Threnody trajectories, diffs and tool JSON, including some 16K+ samples. Measure KLD on a held-out agentic set, never on the calibration data.

KV cache is the second lever. llama.cpp measured **q8_0 K+V at KLD 0.00098, essentially free, against 0.0365 for q4_0 K+V**, and found K much more sensitive than V ([PR #7412](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/7412)). Use `-fa on -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0` as the default. If memory runs short, drop V to q4_0 before touching K. For a pure-attention model these settings decide whether long context fits at all: Qwen3-8B costs about 144 KiB/token, or 18 GiB at 128K in f16. Qwen3.5-9B runs full attention in only one of every four layers (3:1 DeltaNet hybrid), and the DeltaNet layers keep fixed-size state rather than a per-token cache ([Qwen3.5-9B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)). The architecture alone therefore cuts per-token KV by about 4x compared with an equivalent full-attention stack. This is an inference from the layer ratio, not a measured figure. Combined with q8_0 KV, it should let Q6_K weights (about 7.5 GB by bpw arithmetic) and 64K-128K context fit in 12 GB.

MoE offload and pruning matter only if the user moves to the 35B-A3B. Expert offload (`--n-cpu-moe`) delivers the 39 tok/s above, but with 32GB RAM ([insiderllm](https://insiderllm.com/guides/best-way-run-qwen-3-6-35b-moe-locally/)). REAP expert pruning offers a way to shrink it. Cerebras' 20%-pruned **Qwen3-Coder-REAP-25B-A3B kept LiveCodeBench at 35.2 → 35.2 and BFCL-v3 at 63.2 → 62.2** after calibrating on code, function-calling and SWE-smith trajectories ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/cerebras/Qwen3-Coder-REAP-25B-A3B)). The paper reports Δ ≤ 2% on code even at 50% pruning on very large MoEs ([arXiv 2510.13999](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.13999)). A REAP-pruned Qwen3.6-35B-A3B calibrated on Threnody trajectories is the most plausible way to get a 35B-class model running on 16GB RAM, but it has not been tested on that architecture. Minitron-style width pruning needed **94B distillation tokens** to turn an 8B into a 4B ([arXiv 2408.11796](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11796v3)), which is far beyond Colab. QAT recovers about 35-67% of int4 PTQ loss ([PyTorch blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/quantization-aware-training-in-torchao-ii/)). However, Unsloth's dynamic Q4 beat Google's official Gemma 3 QAT file by about 0.8 MMLU points while being 1.6 GB smaller ([Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/unsloth-dynamic-2.0-ggufs)), and TorchAO's int4 format does not map onto K-quants. Skip QAT unless measured Q5 regressions show up.

The total comes to roughly 160-195 CU, leaving a small reserve for one failed run. The SFT and GRPO estimates are inferences from the A100 rate and the note that 1-3K long trajectories at 16K fit in about 26-37 A100-hours. Phase 2 exists to replace those guesses with measured throughput before most of the budget is spent. If the pilot runs slower than expected, cut phase 5 before cutting SFT data.

## Conclusion

This is not mainly a data-scarcity problem. In 2026 there are hundreds of thousands of resolved, permissively licensed agent trajectories, and published curves show gains that keep growing with trajectory count. V4 underperformed because of choices that are cheap to fix: learning rate, loss mask, sequence length and base model. For Threnody, a newer base and a correct recipe will likely produce a larger jump than anything a 200-CU budget could add through clever training. That changes where the effort should go. The highest-value work is curation (length filtering, scaffold matching, identifier-level decontamination) and a larger, trustworthy eval. With 16 tasks, OmniAgent-Bench cannot tell whether Threnody actually improved.

Three open risks deserve explicit tracking. First, Qwen3.5-9B has no published SWE-bench or Terminal-Bench score, so its agentic baseline is unknown until phase 0 measures it. Second, quant sensitivity, TRL template auto-patching, and KV savings for the hybrid DeltaNet architecture were all extrapolated from other models and must be checked on Threnody itself. Third, the 16GB RAM limit is the constraint most worth relaxing. Upgrading to 32GB, which is inexpensive, would unlock the 35B-A3B tier, whose vendor-reported SWE-V of 73.4 is the best available on a 3060. A REAP-pruned variant could then bring that tier within reach of more users.
